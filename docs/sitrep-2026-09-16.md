---

## 1. What the company is

As more work gets done by machines, the bottleneck stops being *doing* the work
and becomes *trusting* it. Verumetric is a machine-only verification layer: it
checks machine-produced work and returns it split into a **certified stream**
(with a measured residual error rate and an honest confidence interval) and an
**exception stream** (could not be established — a human looks at these). No
human at Verumetric touches a customer's job; humans build ground truth and
audit the verifier offline.

The customer is buying the elimination of their checking step, not extraction.

Settled with Tanner 2026-09-16:

- **Scope:** documents first, general underneath. Nothing hardcodes "document."
- **First buyer:** businesses with back-office paper (contractors, suppliers,
  freight brokers) — not consumer one-off jobs.
- **Our role:** we pick the engine *and* check the work — not grading-only.

## 2. What is under test, and the budget

> Can a fully automated evidence stack certify a commercially useful share of
> important fields at a measurable residual error rate, with no human in the
> production path, and without re-running the job through an equally expensive
> model?

**Budget: $100** (cut from $1,000 on 2026-09-16). Staged: $10 pilot (50 pages) →
$60 main run (~350 pages) → $30 reserve. Enforced in code — the ledger refuses
any call that would cross the limit and announces each quarter of budget spent.

Twelve thresholds (T1–T11 + T2b) were pre-registered in `THRESHOLDS.md` **before
any data existed** and are not renegotiated. Two amendments, both pre-data:
T6 (latency) is measure-and-report rather than a gate; T2b routes to a pivot when
the excess residual sits in soft fields and gates when it sits in money/ID;
T5's cost clause gates, its customer-relative clause is pending a real customer
number.

## 3. Data plan — the part most worth challenging

No customer documents exist yet, and **synthetic documents cannot test this
thesis**: generating a page means generating its answer key, so every measured
residual would measure the generator. So the experiment runs on public corpora:

- **CORD** (~1,000 real photographed Indonesian receipts, box-level annotations,
  line items → subtotal → tax → total). Primary. CC BY 4.0.
- **SROIE** (scanned receipts). Secondary. **License unconfirmed** — the ICDAR
  site was unreachable; the download tool refuses to fetch it and requires the
  archive be supplied manually with an explicit acknowledgment flag.
- **FUNSD** (199 noisy forms, no arithmetic) — the recall probe. **Non-commercial
  research use only**: usable to answer the question, not to back any commercial
  claim.

**Contamination is the largest threat to validity.** These corpora are almost
certainly in the training data of the models under test. Memorization would
inflate accuracy and suppress provider divergence, biasing toward a false
"routing has no value" and a false "residual is fine." Defense: every page runs
twice — as-is and augmented (±7° rotation, blur, JPEG 35–60, crop, brightness,
noise, seeded and committed). **All T-metrics come from the augmented run**; the
as-is minus augmented delta per provider is the contamination estimate.

**Gold** is the corpora's published annotations, which contain known errors.
Tanner adjudicates a random 100-field slice; if the annotation error rate exceeds
2% it is folded into every residual as a noise floor. **No residual is reported
below the gold noise floor as though it were real.**

**Two honest limits, stated up front:** no counterpart documents exist in any
public corpus, so cross-document reconciliation — the strongest confirming
evidence in the stack — contributes nothing, and measured coverage is therefore a
**lower bound**. And T8 (customer acceptance) cannot be tested without a
customer, so **a GO from this run is conditional on T8**.

## 4. What is built and proven

4,200 lines of source and tools, 2,100 lines of tests, **218 tests passing**,
9 ADRs. Everything below runs at zero marginal cost, which is why it exists
before any credential does.

- **Claim model** — every value carries provenance (`located` / `derived` /
  `inferred` / `absent`) with per-type validation, a field class, and a
  consequence. Claims are immutable and JSON-round-trip stable.
- **Reference layer** — a word-box OCR pass we own and no provider sees, plus
  grounding: does the claimed text exist, is it where the provider said, and do
  we independently read the same thing there. Three separate signals on purpose.
- **Tier 0 (free)** — type/format, date sanity, checksums, `value parses from
  source_text`, arithmetic reconciliation over class-schema invariants (via a
  restricted AST walk, not `eval`), closed-vocabulary matching, structural recall.
- **Tier 1 (near-free)** — the grounding checks above.
- **Evidence accumulation** — log-odds with a stopping rule: PASS when
  P(wrong) × consequence < the price of the next useful check.
- **Spend ledger** — guards before each call, survives restarts, records failed
  calls.
- **Demo** — renders a synthetic receipt, reads it with a real OCR engine, runs
  four claims (one honest, three defective) through the ladder. All three defects
  caught, each by a different check; honest reading certifies. Runs in ~1s.

**Early quantitative finding:** verification is *not* the expensive part.
At ~12 important fields per receipt, Tier 2 firing on 15% and Tier 3 on 8% costs
about **$0.005/page**, under a third of one extraction pass, because both tiers
are region-scoped. That is the first support for T5 being achievable.

## 5. Bugs the end-to-end run found that unit tests did not

Both would have produced a **confident wrong answer** rather than an obvious
failure. This is the strongest argument for the staged pilot.

1. **Currency.** Consequence is in USD; CORD receipts are in rupiah. Feeding
   66,000 IDR in as 66,000 USD inflates expected loss ~16,000×, sending every
   field to the premium tier. That fails T3 and T5 for a reason with nothing to
   do with verification — the experiment would have concluded "verification is
   uneconomic."
2. **Skew.** Half the corpus is rotated by design. Line grouping worked on raw
   y-coordinates, so on a rotated page every line shattered into fragments (22
   fragments where there are 7 lines). Because grounding only searches spans
   within a line, every *multi-word* field then failed to ground — measured at
   0.67–0.75 against a 0.85 threshold, versus 1.00 once fixed. Single-word fields
   were unaffected, so this would have looked like a plausible accuracy result.

## 6. Blocked on

1. **Cloud OCR credentials** (Textract / Document AI / Azure DI) — the binding
   constraint, and not for cost reasons. T10 requires ≥ 3 provider lineages with
   pairwise error correlation φ < 0.3. We currently have two: our own local OCR,
   and one Claude-family extractor. The premium verifier is also Claude-family,
   so **Tier 3 evidence on a Claude-extracted field is a same-lineage recheck**,
   and its likelihood ratio is held near 1.0 until gold measures the pair.
2. **Anthropic API key** — for the first extractor.
3. **~2 hours of Tanner's time** — adjudicating 100 gold fields.

## 7. What I want attacked

Ranked by how much a wrong answer would cost:

1. **Is the contamination defense sufficient?** Mild augmentation may not defeat
   memorization — a model that memorized a receipt may still recall it from a
   blurred, rotated copy. If so, the as-is/augmented delta *understates*
   contamination and every T-metric is optimistic. Is there a stronger cheap
   test? (Candidate: hold out a corpus published after the models' cutoff.)
2. **Correlated-evidence damping is a guess.** Checks in a correlation group have
   all but the strongest raised to the power 0.3. Multiplying correlated evidence
   as independent understates P(wrong) — the dangerous direction. 0.3 is a
   judgement, not a measurement. Better principled options?
3. **Receipts are not construction tickets.** The commercial target is
   handwritten contractor paper with supplier statements as counterparts. The
   experiment runs on printed/photographed receipts with no counterparts. How
   much does a result here transfer?
4. **Gold noise may swamp the thresholds.** T2 demands a point residual ≤ 0.5%
   against gold that may itself be ~2% wrong. Is the noise-floor rule
   ("never report below it") sufficient, or does it make T2 untestable here?
5. **The stopping rule's exhaustion case.** Spec says UNVERIFIED when checks are
   exhausted. Read literally, a field with P(wrong)=1e-6 and a $5 consequence
   would land in the exception stream because the ladder ran out. I resolved it
   economically: PASS if expected loss is below the price of the cheapest
   escalation we'd have bought, else UNVERIFIED. Is that the right reading?
6. **Sequencing.** Documents first because evidence is densest there and a
   failure is decisive. Counter-argument worth hearing: does that risk building a
   document company by accident?
